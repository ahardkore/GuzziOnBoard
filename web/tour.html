GuzziOnBoard

A safety-first diagnostic workstation for the Magneti Marelli ECUs of Moto Guzzi motorcycles — and the same ECUs as they run in Ducatis and Aprilias. Live data, fault codes, actuator tests, firmware backup and validation, named map tables from TunerPro definitions: everything the laptop can honestly do, and nothing it cannot yet prove.

A walk through the workstation

Click through the views, or use the keyboard: ← →. Every panel below runs against the built-in ECU simulator, so nothing here needs a motorcycle — or risks one.

1 · Garage — pick the bike

Every session starts here, simulator or bike.

Three dropdowns — make, model, year — resolve the ECU and the protocol it speaks. Moto Guzzi, Ducati and Aprilia are covered; cross-brand bikes run read-only until their identifier tables are confirmed on the real machine (the workstation tells you, rather than guessing). The connection panel picks the transport: the built-in simulator, a K-Line USB adapter, or CAN.

2 · Overview — who is this ECU?

First command after connecting, always.

Identification is mandatory before anything else: the ECU answers with its part numbers and software versions, read with service 1A 80. The overview also shows the link state and what this particular ECU supports — capabilities the catalog cannot vouch for are shown greyed out, not hidden.

3 · Live data — with its receipts

Engine running, bike on the stand or at idle in neutral.

Polling decodes every channel the catalog has verified scalings for — RPM, TPS, coolant and air temperature, lambda, battery voltage. The provenance table underneath keeps the raw bytes next to every decoded value: if a scaling is ever wrong, that is the evidence you send with the bug report.

4 · Faults — read, understand, then clear

Any time; clearing asks twice.

Fault memory comes back with SAE and manufacturer descriptions. Clearing is deliberately a two-step action with a typed acknowledgement — the kind of thing you want to be sure about before you erase the evidence.

5 · Service — actuator tests and resets

Engine off, observed, and only for ECU families the catalog trusts.

Injector and relay pulses, throttle balancing hints, TPS resets and self-adaptation wipes — each gated by the confidence level and the observed engine state. The safety gate refuses to command an ECU it does not genuinely understand, and refuses actuator tests while the engine runs.

6 · Firmware — read, verify, validate, and only then write

The one place where patience is a feature.

Reading a 5AM flash takes twenty-plus minutes over K-Line, so a backup is read twice and only kept when both reads agree byte for byte. New images are validated — size, vector table, entropy, hardware family — before a single byte is sent, because flashing an HW1xx image into an HW3xx ECU bricks it. Writing stays behind an explicit acknowledgement, a verified backup and a battery-voltage check.

7 · Maps & tables — a dump with names on it

After a read or with any image on disk.

Feed the workstation a TunerPro XDF definition — the same files the GuzziDiag ecosystem uses, dropped into ~/.guzzionboard/xdfs/ — and a raw dump becomes named fuel and ignition tables with real units. Diff two images and every changed cell is reported by table name and axis value, not by offset. Print or save the tables as a standalone HTML document.

8 · Sessions & reports — the paper trail

Always recording; export when you are done.

Every session is recorded frame by frame — raw bytes and decoded samples — to ~/.guzzionboard/sessions/, exportable as CSV or JSON. The report view assembles a plain-text summary of the whole session: what was connected, what was read, what was commanded and what the safety gate refused.

Setup

From zero to simulator in one command; from simulator to bike with two cables and a battery charger.

1 · Run it (simulator, no hardware)

Any machine with Python 3.11 or newer:

python3 run_server.py  then open http://127.0.0.1:8000. Pick a motorcycle — try Moto Guzzi / Griso 1200 8V / 2012 — connect, and the simulator speaks the real KWP2000 wire protocol back at you.

2 · Talk to a real bike (K-Line)

The 1990s–2010s Marelli ECUs speak KWP2000 over a single-wire K-Line at 10400 baud, through the bike’s 3-pin diagnostic connector:

  • A KKL “VAG-COM 409” USB cable — this is a plain FTDI-based serial adapter with the K-Line driver transistor built in. Get one with a genuine FTDI chip; the clones with fake chips misbehave at 10400 baud.
  • A 3-pin to OBD adapter lead for your bike (Moto Guzzi, Ducati and Aprilia of this era share the same 3-pin diagnostic plug).
  • Set the adapter’s latency timer to 1 ms — the workstation’s adapter pre-flight checks this; the Windows default of 16 ms turns a 20-minute read into hours.

Then: pip install -e '.[hardware]', pick the bike, choose the K-Line transport and your serial port, accept the hardware checklist, connect.

3 · Newer CAN bikes

The CAN-era ECUs (V7 III / V7 850, later V85 TT, V100) wrap the same diagnostics in ISO-TP over CAN. You need a CAN adapter python-can can drive — a USB OBD-II dongle with ELM327-class firmware is the cheap route, a genuine OBDLink or a socketcan interface the reliable one. The request/response CAN identifiers are not yet confirmed on these bikes; the workstation assumes the standard pair and lets you override it per session while the real pair is captured.

4 · Maps from a dump

Download the TunerPro XDF definitions for your ECU from the GuzziDiag archive at von-der-salierburg.de and drop them into ~/.guzzionboard/xdfs/. The Maps & tables view then renders any image — including your verified backups — as named tables, and diffs two images by table and cell.

5 · Before you write anything

  • Battery on a charger or maintainer. A write that loses power mid-flash is a bricked ECU.
  • A verified backup first — the workstation insists, and refuses to write without one.
  • Laptop asleep = ECU bricked. Disable sleep and low-battery actions.
  • Validate the new image against the ECU family first. The workstation does this; read its findings.

KKL VAG-COM 409 USB cable

The K-Line adapter — required for every Marelli ECU from the P8 to the 7SM.

FTDI-based USB serial with the K-Line transceiver built in. Buy one with a genuine FTDI chip and set the latency timer to 1 ms; the workstation's pre-flight walks you through it.

3-pin diagnostic adapter lead

Bike-side plug — Moto Guzzi, Ducati and Aprilia share it.

Converts the round 3-pin diagnostic connector of this era to the OBD socket on the KKL cable. Cheap, and the one cable everyone forgets until the bike is on the stand.

Battery maintainer / charger

Non-negotiable for any read longer than a coffee.

A 20+ minute flash read or a write session needs a stable supply. A maintainer with a proper charging profile keeps the battery voltage in the window the safety gate checks before any write.

OBDLink SX / MX+ (CAN era)

For the CAN bikes: V7 III, V7 850, later V85 TT, V100.

ISO-TP over CAN needs a responsive adapter; the OBDLink family has the timing to match. STN-based adapters also give you a fallback CLI for identifier sniffing, which is exactly what the CAN families still need.

USB multi-meter or clamp meter

Trust, but verify — the cheap insurance.

Verifying battery voltage at the ECU connector before a write, and checking that the K-Line idle voltage is where it should be, catches most “the tool is broken” sessions before they start.

Anti-static mat & wrist strap

If the ECU comes off the bike and onto the bench.

Bench work — key captures, memory reads on a loose ECU — is how this project’s unverified families get promoted. A grounded mat costs less than one ECU.
